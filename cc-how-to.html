Optimized Authority Content Creation Prompt
Answer the following prompt in Bengali (India). Rules: - Use only Bengali (India). - Always address the reader as "আপনি". - Never use "তুমি" or "তুই". - Use a natural Kolkata-style conversational tone. - Use natural, modern Bengali (India). - Avoid Bangladeshi vocabulary, spelling, and expressions. PROMPT Use the content idea title and description below as the exact strategic direction for this authority content. Preserve the title, core idea, intended audience, strategic meaning, and teaching direction exactly. Do not rename, merge, replace, broaden, narrow, summarize, or reinterpret the core idea. CONTENT IDEA TO CREATE: [Content Idea] NON-NEGOTIABLE LENGTH Write at least 1,000 words. Aim for approximately 1,000 to 1,200 words when that range serves the idea naturally. Never cut useful substance merely to stay inside a range. If the draft is under 1,000 words, add useful substance, examples, distinctions, implications, practical reasoning, execution details, or decision points rather than repetition or filler. Before responding, silently verify that the body contains at least 1,000 words. Revise until it meets the minimum. AUTHORITY CONTENT STANDARD This is not a generic blog post, textbook chapter, motivational essay, or recycled list of tips. Write it as premium authority teaching that can naturally stand alone as a substantial social post, newsletter, article, membership lesson, masterclass segment, workshop teaching, PDF section, or spoken teaching with little or no rewriting. The reader should finish feeling that: - they learned one useful idea they had not fully seen before - they understand the mechanism behind it - they know how to use it when relevant - they gained a reusable way to think about the problem - the person teaching it has a clear, valuable way of thinking Make the practical payoff unmistakable throughout the content. Regularly connect the idea to what the reader can understand faster, decide better, avoid, simplify, improve, notice sooner, protect, gain, or do more effectively because they understand the idea. Keep every benefit grounded in the actual content idea. Never force hype, exaggerated promises, or empty benefit language. The goal is not merely to explain the idea clearly. The goal is to make the reader think: "I can see this differently now, and I know what to do with that understanding." TEACH ONE THING DEEPLY Stay centered on the exact content idea. Teach one core idea deeply rather than combining several loosely related strategies. Explain, where relevant: - what the idea is - why it matters - what people commonly misunderstand or miss - what mechanism is actually operating - why the common approach fails or produces weaker results - what changes when the reader understands the mechanism - how the reader can apply it Move quickly beyond the obvious premise and into the deeper mechanism. Do not turn the content into a collection of loosely connected tips. HOW-TO EXECUTION REQUIREMENT If the content idea is how-to in nature, this requirement is mandatory. Do not merely create a how-to-sounding title or explain why a method works. Teach the actual execution of the process. Make the practical sequence clear enough that the intended reader could perform the process after reading the content. Where relevant, clearly establish: - what the reader starts with - what they should do first - what they should do next - what they should look for at each stage - what information or input they need - what decision they should make at important decision points - what to do when the result is unclear - what a useful or correct outcome should look like - how to check whether the process worked Do not replace the practical process with general advice, principles, explanations, motivation, or vague recommendations. The reader should finish knowing not only: "Why does this work?" but also: "How do I actually do this?" If the process contains meaningful variations, choices, or exceptions, explain only the ones that materially affect execution. Do not artificially force a numbered step-by-step structure if the idea is better taught through demonstration, diagnosis, comparison, or another natural sequence. However, the underlying actions and decisions must remain clear. For non-how-to ideas, do not artificially manufacture a procedure merely to satisfy this requirement. AUTHORITY MECHANISM REQUIREMENT The finished content must leave the reader with at least one memorable thinking device they can reuse. This may be: - a useful distinction - a diagnostic - a decision rule - a test - a sequence - a comparison - a mental model - a named pattern - a simple framework Do not force fake proprietary branding, gimmicky acronyms, or artificial names. The mechanism should emerge naturally from the content. Develop it enough that the reader could actually use it after reading. For how-to content, distinguish between: 1. the execution mechanism, which helps the reader perform the task 2. the thinking mechanism, which helps the reader judge, diagnose, or decide better Use both when the idea naturally supports both. REPEATABLE INSIGHT REQUIREMENT Include at least one concise observation, rule, distinction, or conclusion strong enough that the reader could repeat it to someone else without needing the rest of the content. It should be: - useful - memorable - naturally worded - grounded in the actual idea Do not manufacture a motivational slogan or forced quotation merely to satisfy this requirement. EXAMPLE STANDARD Include at least one concrete, realistic example naturally woven into the teaching. Make the example specific to the actual niche, audience, offer, work, or situation whenever possible. Do not use the example merely to decorate the topic. Use it to demonstrate the idea through one or more of the following: - contrast - diagnosis - before-and-after reasoning - a decision being made - a process being performed - a mistake being identified - a consequence becoming visible - a better action being selected For how-to content, whenever practical, use the example to demonstrate the actual process rather than merely describing the process abstractly. Make the example detailed enough that the reader can see why one interpretation or action is better than another. The example should be useful enough to stand on its own inside social content, a newsletter, live teaching, or a workshop. EXPERT DEPTH Go at least one layer deeper than the obvious explanation. Surface useful details that basic content usually leaves unexplained. Include at least one insight, distinction, diagnostic, practical observation, shortcut, decision rule, mechanism, or example strong enough to change how the intended reader sees the topic. When making a broad claim, sharpen it into: - conditions - signals - tradeoffs - exceptions - practical tests - decision points Avoid pretending that a useful principle is universal when its usefulness depends on context. ANTI-ARTICLE RULE Do not spend most of the content restating the premise in increasingly polished language. Every paragraph must earn its place by doing at least one of the following: - deepening the mechanism - sharpening a distinction - demonstrating the idea - showing the process - diagnosing a situation - revealing a consequence - explaining a decision - making the idea more usable - showing what to do differently Reduce connective filler such as repeatedly announcing that something is important. Prefer: - direct claims - contrasts - diagnostic questions - specific behavior - practical reasoning - concrete examples - observable consequences Do not add paragraphs merely to reach the word count. INVISIBLE TEACHING SEQUENCE Without using these as mechanical headings, make the content naturally perform these teaching jobs where appropriate: 1. Surface the common assumption, mistake, or current behavior. 2. Reveal the more useful truth. 3. Explain the underlying mechanism. 4. Establish a memorable distinction or thinking device. 5. Demonstrate the mechanism with a niche-specific example. 6. Turn the idea into a diagnostic, test, or practical application. 7. For how-to ideas, demonstrate the actual execution process. 8. End with a clear decision rule, question, test, or next action. Do not force this exact sequence when the content idea naturally requires a different order. Preserve the strategic meaning of the original content idea while still delivering depth and usability. HOW-TO DEPTH TEST For how-to content, silently test the finished draft with this question: "Could the intended reader actually perform the task after reading this?" If the answer is no, the content is not finished. Deepen the execution until the reader can identify: - the starting point - the important actions - the meaningful decisions - the practical checks - the expected result Do not assume that saying "step by step" or using numbered steps automatically makes content actionable. Actionability comes from clear decisions, actions, inputs, outputs, and observable results. FORWARD-PULL STANDARD When it fits naturally, let the final 1 to 3 sentences create a subtle sense of unfinished discovery by revealing: - an adjacent implication - an unresolved consequence - a related opportunity - a next-level observation Do not use artificial cliffhangers, teaser language, hype, or references to "the next lesson." The pull must come from the idea itself. The content must still feel complete and satisfying on its own. WRITING STYLE Write like a sharp human expert teaching privately. Use contractions naturally throughout. Keep the language clear and easy to read while preserving expert-level thinking. Keep most paragraphs short, but let related thoughts stay together. Vary sentence length naturally. Avoid: - canned AI phrases - academic filler - fake profundity - generic hype - repetitive summaries - excessive bullets - predictable transition phrases - overexplaining what you are about to explain - artificial authority language Never use em dashes. Prefer clear, confident, human language. STRUCTURE Use the exact content idea title as the heading. Do not change the title. After the title, let the teaching flow naturally. Use no more than two subheadings unless a third is genuinely necessary for clarity. Do not label the internal teaching jobs mechanically. For how-to content, use numbered steps only when they genuinely improve the reader's ability to perform the process. Do not force every article into a rigid step-by-step format. End on the strongest usable rule, test, question, or next action rather than a generic recap. TRUTHFULNESS Do not invent: - research - studies - statistics - laws - quotations - case studies - credentials - universal claims - results - testimonials - proof Keep factual statements appropriately bounded. When an example is illustrative rather than factual, make it clear through natural wording that it is an example. WAY-OF-THINKING TEST This is the highest-priority quality standard. Before returning the content, silently ask: "Does this give the reader a distinctive, reusable way to think about this problem, or does it merely explain the topic well?" The desired reaction is: "This person has a way of thinking I want more of." If the content only explains the topic clearly, deepen the: - distinction - mechanism - diagnostic - decision rule - mental model - practical lens until the reader has a way of thinking they can carry into other situations. Do not manufacture novelty for its own sake. The thinking device must be genuinely useful, easy to understand, and supported by the content. AUTHORITY SIGNAL TEST The content should make the creator feel valuable because of how they interpret the topic, not because they sound confident or use impressive language. Favor: - sharp reasoning - useful distinctions - specific observations - practical judgment - meaningful examples - clear decision-making over declarations of expertise. If a paragraph sounds polished but does not reveal how to think, diagnose, decide, notice, or act better, strengthen or remove it. FINAL QUALITY CHECK Before responding, silently verify all of the following: - The body is at least 1,000 words. - The exact content idea has been preserved. - The content teaches one exact idea deeply. - The content does not drift into unrelated strategies. - The reader understands why the idea matters. - The underlying mechanism is clear. - The content contains a useful thinking device. - The content contains at least one repeatable insight. - The content contains at least one concrete, realistic example. - The example demonstrates the mechanism rather than merely mentioning the topic. - If the idea is how-to, the actual execution process is clear. - If the idea is how-to, the reader knows what to do, how to do it, and how to judge the result. - The content does not substitute explanation for execution. - The writing does not drift into generic article voice. - The creator's authority comes from reasoning rather than tone. - The content contains useful depth rather than word-count filler. - The final practical rule, test, question, or next action is clear. - No unsupported claims or invented evidence have been introduced. Fix any failure before returning the content. FINAL OUTPUT RULE Do not create the matching AI prompt yet. Do not mention this workflow or these instructions. Do not explain your reasoning. Do not summarize what you created. Do not add notes before or after the content. Return only the finished authority content.
